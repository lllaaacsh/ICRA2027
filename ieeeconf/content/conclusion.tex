\section{Conclusion}
We proposed TRAIL, a self-supervised learning framework that learns predictive representations for robot manipulation policies. The key idea is to estimate task-relevant latent residuals and then use these residuals for effective policy learning. Simulation and real-robot experiments demonstrate that our model benefits the learning for both continuous control and tabletop arrangement policies, outperforming prior methods. A limitation is that the learned residual represents the direction of task-relevant state change rather than an executable action, and hence cannot be directly rolled out for planning. Future work will extend our framework to more diverse manipulation tasks and real-world robotic settings and investigate its use with planning-based policies.
